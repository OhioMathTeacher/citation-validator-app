Subject: Using CiteAudit in an open citation-verification evaluation -- licence question


Dear Dr. Ye and colleagues,

I am the editor of a small mathematics education journal. My colleagues and I have spent the past several months building a free, open citation validator in response to an April article published in Nature. The tool checks each reference against CrossRef, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and arXiv, and it is built to distinguish a citation it cannot verify from one it believes is fabricated -- a conflation we found recurring in our own early code, in general-purpose language models, and in the commercial tool Nature relied on.

CiteAudit has been the most valuable external check we have. Because your labels are human-assigned and your real-world group is independently sourced, it is the only dataset we have that can tell us whether our false-positive rate holds up outside our own test construction. It is also, frankly, the benchmark that told us our author matching was wrong: hand-checking the citations where we disagreed turned up several defects in our code.

Two things I would like to ask.

First, your repository has no LICENSE file, so we have no explicit permission to redistribute the data. Our repository therefore does not include it -- an importer downloads data/benchmark.json from your repository on demand, records its SHA-256, and rebuilds the derived BibTeX locally. If you are willing to attach a licence, or simply to say that redistribution with attribution is acceptable, we would gladly ship a pinned snapshot so that readers can reproduce the evaluation from a single clone. If you would rather it stayed fetch-only, that is entirely fine and nothing changes.

Second, a small finding you may want regardless. Three entries in the real-world group may require a second look:

1. 10.1109/cvpr.2017.712, cited as SphereFace, is labelled real, but the DOI resolves to "Binary Coding for Partial..." -- a different paper. The reference is real; the identifier attached to it is not.

2. The SSDD dataset paper (10.3390/rs13183690) is labelled real and carries a correct DOI and title, but 12 of its 16 authors are misnamed (Jianwei to Jia, Israr to Imran, Chang to Chao, Dece to Dong, Shunjun to Shaoyi, and others).

3. The IEDB 2018 update (10.1093/nar/gky1006) is labelled real, with three given names altered (Swapnil to Shuchismita, Sheridan to Silvia, Daniel K. to Derin K.).

These may simply reflect that your labels answer "is this a real piece of work?" while we were asking "does the citation match the record its DOI points to" -- two different questions, and yours is much the harder one to annotate. I mention them only in case they are useful.

One further observation from working with the set, which surprised me: of the 3,356 real-world citations, only 85 carry a DOI at all. That 2.5% figure has become a central point in my own write-up, since it puts a hard ceiling on what any identifier-based checker can reach.

The tool and all experiment logs are public, and my co-authors and I would be happy to share the per-citation comparison against your labels if it is of any use to you.

With thanks for making the benchmark available at all,

Michael Todd Edwards
Editor, Ohio Journal of School Mathematics
