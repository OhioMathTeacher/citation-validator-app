Subject: CiteAudit redistribution -- a correction to what I told you on 4 August

Dear Dr. Ye and colleagues,

Following up on my 4 August note about redistributing CiteAudit: I told you our repository did not include your data, only fetched it on demand. That was true of the data directory and not true of the repository as a whole, and I would rather you heard it from me.

Auditing our own repository yesterday, I found that the per-citation result files from our experiments had recorded each citation's author, title, identifier and original reference string. Your real-world group, and the subset of it we run most often, were therefore reproducible from our results directory even though the datasets themselves were withheld. This went back to 21 May.

Those files now keep only our tool's verdict for each citation -- the status, which registry resolved it, and the model's judgement -- with the citation text removed, and we have added a test that fails if it returns. Every release archived on Zenodo before yesterday -- seven of them, v1.4.0 through v1.6.0 -- still contains the unredacted files; Zenodo records are immutable, so those cannot be altered. Zenodo's download counter stands at zero for all seven, so as best I can tell nobody has ever obtained your data this way. Our git history likewise retains them, and rewriting it would break permalinks cited in a forthcoming article.

Nothing is required of you. If you would prefer we take any further step with the archived releases, I will do it. The standing question from 4 August is also still open, and still entirely yours to decline: if redistribution with attribution were acceptable we would ship a pinned snapshot, and if not, fetch-only remains completely fine.

With apologies for the correction and thanks for the benchmark,

Michael Todd Edwards
Editor, Ohio Journal of School Mathematics
